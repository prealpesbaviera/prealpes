The Warm-Up Before Things Get Interesting

It had been a very cloudy week, so it was hard to tell what the ground conditions were actually like up on the summits. After comparing information from several weather apps, one thing became clear: heading out early in the morning simply wasn't worth it.

So we planned to leave home at around 11. And sure enough, we set off at 11:15. One of these days, we'll manage to be on time.

Just before noon, we arrived at the small car park on Prinzenweg, which has room for barely 15 cars. So during the high season, don't even bother trying your luck there. If you're coming from Munich, you can take the train to Tegernsee and walk from the station in about ten minutes.

And even if you do find a parking space, it's fairly expensive: around eight euros. Since I'm a firm believer in spending as little money as possible, we managed to find a perfect free spot near some roadworks on Prinzenweg. Being Sunday, there was absolutely no trouble.

I'd say roughly 70% of the ascent follows a wide gravel road through the forest. I'm not particularly fond of those kinds of tracks, but if you have a good mountain bike and excellent fitness, you could cycle a considerable part of the climb.

The hike only starts getting interesting when you leave the gravel road behind and head up towards the summit along trails full of exposed roots. That's where your fitness really starts to matter.

Walking Among Roots, Mud and Clouds

Climbing steep, root-covered trails is actually one of my strengths. But when everything is wet, it becomes a real challenge – and a dangerous one at that.

Wet tree roots are a bit like your hands after washing them: they become smooth and slippery. So watch carefully where you put your feet. One wrong step and you could easily slip and twist an ankle.

In some sections, the trail runs right along the edge of steep drops, where a single slip could be fatal. Good hiking boots with proper grip and paying close attention to every step are essential if you want to make it through the day in one piece – or, for that matter, make it through the day at all.

Of course, I don't spend every step thinking it might be my last. I simply concentrate on getting it right and enjoying the experience.

The higher we climbed during those final 45 minutes, the thicker the fog became. Fortunately, it's a very popular trail, so the chances of getting lost are fairly slim.

As we approached the summit, we could barely see anything. Not being able to see what lies ahead creates a strange uncertainty that gradually wears you down.

Luckily, I was trying out a new watch that displays altitude, so at least I knew we couldn't have much farther to go.

And then, just twenty vertical metres below the summit, the clouds suddenly parted, revealing the most wonderful sunshine.

As always, there's a world of difference between seeing something, photographing it, recording a video of it – and actually experiencing it for yourself.

Absolutely sensational! One moment we were surrounded by fog, and the next everything was bathed in sunshine. We were standing above the clouds, my shadow projected onto them inside a rainbow, surrounded by a view so extraordinary it left me speechless.

From the Summit of Baumgartenschneid to the Riederstein Rock

On the same mountain massif lies another remarkable summit called Riederstein, where a small chapel stands on top of an enormous rock.

It's one of the most famous attractions around Tegernsee. Normally, you have to queue for quite a while to get that spectacular photograph overlooking the lake.

The good news? We didn't have to queue at all.

The bad news? We couldn't see a thing. Everything was covered in fog.

Moments like these teach you to appreciate the gifts the mountains give you. The mountain gives you what it wants to give you, whenever it chooses.

Once we'd made our way down, the sky cleared completely. It was simply a matter of an hour and a half.

But staying up there long enough to enjoy those views would have meant descending in the dark.

Oh well. The photographs speak for themselves.

Heading Home

There's plenty to do in Tegernsee, of course. It's a very touristy town, home to quite a few celebrities, and generally a lovely place.

One place you really shouldn't miss is the Bräustüberl. It's my kind of BBB outing: bueno, bonito y barato – good, nice and inexpensive. You can enjoy a proper meal and drink my favourite beer, Tegernseer, which shares its name with the town.

This time, though, we headed straight home.

Although I didn't find the mountain particularly demanding, the hike takes quite a long time – five hours in our case. And eventually, those hours catch up with you.

At that point, a nice hot bath sounds like a much better plan.
